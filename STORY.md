# The story, as the owner tells it

The owner's arc (24.10.2026). It replaces the design document's plan for Release 2 (its Acts III and IV,
the Studios and the Lawyers, and its finale at the top of the Chart). Release 1 already follows it.
`DECISIONS.md` D-045 to D-047 and D-050 to D-052 record what changed and why.

## What is wrong with the world

The Producers' Guild found one sound that works on everybody. They tested it, polished it, measured it,
and every few months they let a new song out that swallows everything else on the air. No more originals,
only formulas. Musicians fought back with songs they meant; the Guild had more speakers than they had
songs.

While a hook loops, no other music gets through. The swans live on song: cut off from theirs, they fade.
So does Bobbin, whose notes are his strength.

The cure is originality. When people go back to their own sound they are free of the hook, and every time
Bobbin helps someone get there, he takes back one of his notes.

## The Song of Originality (owner direction, built)

In Act II, deep in the Hit Factory, Juno warns Bobbin off the bottom drawer of the Preset Library: Preset
Zero, "corrupted", though it hums. Bobbin has to do something and find something:

1. He weaves **Unmasking** on the library. The labels (SAD 4, HAPPY 12, ANTHEMIC (MILD)) come off like
   masks: under every one, the same few bars, cut and cut again, all from one older song, and every cut
   leads down to the drawer with no label.
2. He weaves **Opening** on Preset Zero. Inside is a wax cylinder: a voice, a fiddle and a clapping room, a
   tune nobody owns. It is the **Song of Originality**, the thing the Guild cut every preset from and then
   locked away. Played whole, it makes everyone who hears it remember their own sound, all at once, the
   flock too.
3. The Distaff knows it and tries to answer, but the song climbs to the high C and back: eight notes, and he
   can play four. The lost sockets only spark.
4. He understands the road: the missing notes aren't in any drawer. They come back as sparks, one each time
   someone finds their own sound. That is why he travels from world to world helping people back to their
   originality: helping them is how he helps himself.

He won't go through the factory's gate until he has found it, and from then on the Book of Patterns keeps a
page for the song: all eight notes, lit as far as the Distaff can play them, a spark for each still to win.

## The journey: worlds of music

Bobbin travels through the city's music, helping one world at a time back to its own sound, and the
world comes together as he goes.

The order is the owner's (24.10.2026): pop first, because it is the most formulaic; then rock; then K-pop,
because it is formula perfected.

- **Release 1 (built):** Act I, Pop (the Starlight Spire, Lyra, LYRIX), and Act II, the Hit Factory (the
  Producers' production floor under the city, Juno, Dominic), where the formulas are made.
- **Release 2 (to build):** Act III, Rock (a), and Act IV, K-pop (b). The high C waits for the finale.

**K-pop (owner direction):** at its heart is a girl who wants to be a K-pop star and doesn't meet the
agency's standards: the wrong height, the wrong voice, a dance she can't stop making her own. The agency
measures everything and she never fits. Bobbin helps her stop trying to fit: her own sound, her own moves,
and the note b comes back. The satire is at the standards and the agency that answers to the Chairman, never
at her or at the idols.

## The Chairman

The head of the Producers' Guild is a new character, the Chairman: a white suit, dark glasses indoors, silver
hair, and a gold pin with the Guild's seal, a hook inside a ring. He doesn't sign singers; he signs sounds.
He is hinted at all through the game (owner direction):

- **His seal** is on every billboard and poster in the city: the street billboard, the Spire's sign, the
  factory poster, the arena poster. The street billboard's description points it out.
- **He meets Bobbin early (built).** The first time Bobbin walks into the Starlight Spire, the Chairman
  steps out of the elevator, plays him a demo from a gold phone (three bars of hook and one empty bar, "where
  you go") and offers a contract. Bobbin can say no, ask about the swans, or reach for the pen; the pen won't
  stay in his hand and the Distaff hums one sour note. Bobbin refuses: "A song isn't something you own. It's
  something you mean." The Chairman: "Keep your sound, then. I'll write something this whole city can't get
  out of its head. You included." That refusal is why the finale's hit exists.
- **Others talk about him.** The receptionist has never seen him come down to the lobby. Dominic, at the end
  of Act II: "The Chairman owns the masters. He owns the notes." After the encore his voice comes over every
  speaker in the dome: "Not bad, little weaver. Now I'm listening." The end card says the same.
- **Release 2:** more of him, never for long: a voice on a PA in the rock world, a car that pulls away, a
  K-pop agency that answers to him.

**The Monotone stays** (owner direction): the hunger from the design document that slipped through behind
the flock. It is the voice behind the Chairman. His signature under every line is already one low note,
repeated. When the hook breaks in the finale, what is left in the speakers is that one flat note: the
Monotone, which has been singing through him.

## The finale

It happens at the top of the Producers' Guild tower, inside their building: the tower from Marcus's memory,
whose crown broadcasts every new hook to the city. Bobbin climbs it, and the last fight is in the room at
the very top, where the songs go out.

The Chairman is furious. He releases the catchiest song there has ever been: the four bars he promised in the
lobby.

Everyone Bobbin helped falls under it again: Lyra, Juno, Marcus, the rockers, the K-pop idols, and his own
flock, the swans' choir, Skein too. Bobbin sees them all greyed, swaying on the beat.

Bobbin has three attempts to play long notes: held notes, a new way of playing the Distaff, that braid
the different styles together. What he plays is the Song of Originality, whole at last now that the high C
is back, carrying every style he gathered on the way. Each attempt brings back more. With the last, a burst of creativity
breaks the hook: one by one, the people he helped join in, each in their own style, until the whole world
is free of the Producers. The swans' choir comes back last, and Skein sings her first note.

As everywhere in this game, nothing is lost by failing: a missed attempt costs only a retry, and Skein
helps after a few.

**What becomes of the Chairman (owner direction):** the burst wakes his own people too: the assistants,
the producers and the scouts who ran the formula for him. They hear the originals the way everyone else
does, and they want to sign the songwriters, the musicians and the singers with their own music. Nobody
wants his four bars any more, and nobody works for him. That is how he loses his power: not beaten, just
left behind, with one flat note in the speakers.

## The ending

The swans are free and white again. They lift off and go on with their journey through the stars, and
Bobbin flies with them.
